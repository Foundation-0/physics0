# Geometry, recurrence and independent constraints

Research note, 12 November 2026. This note connects the proposed lattice, orbit and atomic-spectrum work to the Foundation0 convergence study. Its numerical examples use declared models. They establish no material phase transition, new gravitational orbit or newly identified atomic level.

## A fixed graph can move through different geometries

The incentre-lattice probe preserves the original five-neighbor bar graph along a fold whose endpoint has fcc sphere positions and twelve geometric contacts per site. A scalar Ising model with a fixed coupling on those original bars has the same partition function throughout the fold by construction. Adding the new contacts defines another model.

A scattering pattern can change even for unchanged spin correlations because measured positions enter its structure factor.

The framework family has prior art in [Borcea and Streinu, Section 7](https://doi.org/10.1177/1081286520948116). That paper uses a different bar-length ratio and does not establish the particular equal-bar curve used by the probe.

An independent convergence calculation places identical central springs on the original bars. Their vibrational spectrum changes during the fold although the sum of squared frequencies stays fixed. It fixes the periodic cell, repeats displacements identically in every cell and omits new contact springs. It does not establish full mechanical stability or CaB6 material properties.

The spring calculation uses the standard relation between bond extensions and the dynamical matrix described by [Kane and Lubensky, equations 1–2](https://arxiv.org/html/1308.0554v2).

## Returning shape need not return orientation

A prescribed three-mass shape loop returns its internal distances while accumulating 27 degrees of orientation at zero total angular momentum. Reversing the loop reverses the angle.

This illustrates established geometric-phase mechanics, rather than a freely evolving Newtonian orbit. [Montgomery's primary treatment](https://arxiv.org/pdf/dg-ga/9510005) explains reconstruction of orientation from a three-body shape curve.

Orbit certification must therefore state closure up to rotation, particle permutation and time shift before counting distinct families. Existence and stability remain separate questions.

## Spectral agreement needs independent calibration

Several atomic transitions can constrain one energy level, but dense spectra permit accidental combinations and shared calibration errors. [The level-search study, Sections 2.2–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.16184v1) requires multiple matching lines and evaluates candidate counts using line density and uncertainty.

A successful arithmetic constraint check alone does not identify the ion or validate the wavelength scale.

## Next tests

- Compare fixed-bar spins, central-spring modes and smooth distance-dependent couplings along the same fold. Specify boundaries and observables. Use susceptibility and spectral moments before fitting a critical point.
- Reconstruct orientation for an existing three-body trajectory from its shape curve. Retain particle labels and symmetries, then test full-state return and stability separately.
- Infer a known atomic level from selected lines and predict withheld transitions. Compare against null spectra preserving local line density, uncertainty, allowed quantum numbers and search range. Test another exposure or calibration to expose shared wavelength errors.

# New results across the Foundation-0 research threads

The wider refresh finds a substantive extension of the geometry work and a new observational screen. It also exposes why source-pattern checks cannot establish independent verification. The strongest emerging connection remains precise: two descriptions that agree on selected summaries can differ on the observable or evidential claim of interest.

This was a research survey of 41 accessible repositories, with main heads recorded at 2026-11-13 03:08 UTC. It was not an exhaustive scientific review of every repository or open branch. Recent pushes often concerned unmerged work. Only exoplanets-frontier, vitruvian3d and gravity had main commits dated November 12–13 in that snapshot.

## Geometry adds a controlled transport distinction

The [Vitruvian review](https://github.com/Foundation-0/vitruvian3d/pull/9) finds that the same prescribed bar graph and occupied fraction can coexist with different accessible directions for a probe. Certified periodic routes span two directions in one configuration and three in another. Covered planes supply the obstruction in the first configuration.

This strengthens the earlier graph-versus-geometry comparison. Controlling density does not determine access through the complement of the structure. Winding rank is the relevant geometric observable here. It is distinct from local dimension, spectral dimension and fractal dimension. The matched-density endpoints do not establish a constant-volume path between them.

The layer construction gives another useful distinction. A one-cell switching barrier need not remain a nonzero expansion fraction in large cells. Sequential conversion of N layers requires a fixed absolute excess volume, so its excess per motif scales as 1/N. The period vector moves. A fixed exterior instead prohibits the specified finitely supported translations.

The fixed-angle mechanical instability remains intact under its own assumptions. A feasible geometric path supplies no energy law, stable material or gravitational dynamics. The geometry results do justify a focused next question about transport under an explicitly specified constitutive law.

## Observations need provenance and calibration

The [Issue 5 audit](https://github.com/Foundation-0/exoplanets-frontier/pull/6) confirms that the saved screen rejects six candidate signals belonging to four stars, plus five comparison signals. All 40 result-manifest hashes match. These are reproducible ledger facts, not a repeat of the light-curve analysis or a statement that the stars lack planets.

The source exposes a sharper issue: `known_nasa` can arise from the upstream candidate list itself. An executable control reproduces this with empty external catalogs. That flag therefore cannot serve as independent confirmation. The screen's habitable-zone label also lacks an explicit habitable-zone acceptance condition.

A bounded follow-up now demonstrates a sensitivity limit. The unchanged seeded search recovers a fixed 25-day synthetic transit with corrected MES 49.76 but SDE 7.23, below its 7.5 acceptance gate. A 17-day control passes. A separate lead replay reproduces the saved output. This tests one gate, not survey completeness, and does not rescue any real candidate.

This connects to the KMOS acceleration analysis through inference, not through a common physical mechanism. In KMOS, velocity and gas calibration can absorb an apparent redshift trend. Here catalog lineage and the screen's selection function determine what a rejection or successful crossmatch can establish.

## Independent checking needs a mathematical contract

The new Python auditor in [formal-claims-audit](https://github.com/Foundation-0/formal-claims-audit/pull/16) motivated a bounded adversarial review. Its original script and test did not parse. After isolating the unchanged function definitions, two invalid acceptance stubs with a no-op difference received an apparent independence pass.

Distinct syntax cannot establish separate reasoning or mathematical correctness. Even independently written checkers can encode the same mistaken model. Useful evidence includes explicit acceptance contracts, different representations, controls aimed at shared mistakes, and source bindings. Each establishes its particular scope.

The [stacked scanner repair](https://github.com/Foundation-0/formal-claims-audit/pull/17) fixes execution and binding-scope handling, preserves the counterexamples, and labels unsupported comparisons inconclusive. Its 36 tests and separate scientific review pass. A clean scan reports only that its selected patterns were not detected.

The connected [planning proposal](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/blob/f6d42123287b58e98801e6e5d77b384fcb0099ac/deep-fields/scouting/combinatorial-planning-verified-bar.md) illustrates a separate mathematical obligation. Edge inequalities for a potential bound the path cost only after a goal boundary is supplied.

A constant potential of 35 on a one-edge graph satisfies every edge inequality but does not make its shortest path cost 35.

For every modeled transition, require `h(s) <= cost(s,s') + h(s')`. At every relevant goal require `h(goal) <= 0`. Telescoping then gives `h(start) <= total path cost`. Coverage of the intended states, transitions and goals remains essential. This requirement correction does not disprove any underlying Sokoban record.

The [planning correction](https://github.com/Foundation-0/f0/pull/37) now supplies these missing conditions on the proposal's own branch. The original author branch is preserved.

## Older work suggests methods, not new physical conclusions

The existing rigid-realizations work uses exact interval certificates for framework realizations. Point-charges uses interval verification of equilibrium counts. These are relevant techniques for later mechanical or gravitational equilibrium questions, but transferring a method requires a separately specified model and certificate.

The finite-state-proofs work makes the semantic obligation explicit: a checked state model must still represent the intended counting problem. The relative-equilibria and certified-orbits repositories describe planned work and state that it has not started. None of these metadata observations establishes a new gravity result.
